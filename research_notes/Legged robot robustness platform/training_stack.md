# Training Stack for Fast, Robust Quadruped Locomotion (MuJoCo + PPO, CPU-oriented)

Research method note: arxiv.org, alphaxiv.org, ar5iv, playground.mujoco.org and cdn.jsdelivr.net were blocked by this session's network policy, so I could not read paper PDFs directly. The most load-bearing numbers below come from the **actual source code** instead: legged_gym config (raw GitHub), MuJoCo Playground v0.2.0 (PyPI wheel `playground==0.2.0`, files `_src/locomotion/go1/joystick.py`, `go1/randomize.py`, `config/locomotion_params.py`), Brax 0.14.2 PPO defaults (PyPI wheel), Gymnasium 1.0.0 `ant_v5.py`, and walk-these-ways configs (raw GitHub). Code is a primary source and is more reliable than paper summaries. Note: Playground `1.0.1` on PyPI has been restructured (robots/, rl/), and the Go1 env files live in the 0.x layout.

## Q1. PPO hyperparameters and tricks for fast quadruped convergence

### Takeaway
Two configs have been tested on real hardware and are worth copying. **legged_gym/rsl_rl**: γ=0.99, λ=0.95, clip 0.2, 5 epochs × 4 minibatches, lr 1e-3 with adaptive-KL schedule (target KL 0.01), entropy 0.01, 24 steps/env per iteration, ELU MLP [512,256,128], init std 1.0. **Playground/Brax Go1**: γ=0.97, λ=0.95, lr 3e-4, entropy 1e-2, unroll 20, 32 minibatches × 4 updates, observation normalization on, asymmetric critic with privileged state. Both rely on massively parallel envs (4096–8192). On CPU you get far fewer envs, so the per-sample tricks matter more: obs normalization, asymmetric critic, adaptive LR, and PD targets around a nominal pose.

### Cited Findings
- **legged_gym PPO defaults** (`LeggedRobotCfgPPO`): clip_param 0.2, entropy_coef 0.01, num_learning_epochs 5, num_mini_batches 4, learning_rate 1e-3, schedule 'adaptive', gamma 0.99, lam 0.95, desired_kl 0.01, max_grad_norm 1.0, value_loss_coef 1.0; policy init_noise_std 1.0, actor/critic [512,256,128], activation 'elu'; runner num_steps_per_env 24, max_iterations 1500; num_envs 4096 — [legged_gym legged_robot_config.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leggedrobotics/legged_gym/master/legged_gym/envs/base/legged_robot_config.py)
- **legged_gym obs scaling (manual normalization)**: obs_scales lin_vel 2.0, ang_vel 0.25, dof_pos 1.0, dof_vel 0.05, height_measurements 5.0; clip_observations 100, clip_actions 100; control decimation 4, action_scale 0.5, PD control_type 'P'; episode_length_s 20 — [legged_gym config](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leggedrobotics/legged_gym/master/legged_gym/envs/base/legged_robot_config.py)
- **MuJoCo Playground RSL-RL config** (used with MJX/Warp envs): entropy_coef 0.001, lr 3e-4, schedule "fixed", gamma 0.99, lam 0.95, clip 0.2, 5 epochs, 4 minibatches, use_clipped_value_loss True, num_steps_per_env 24, **empirical_normalization=True** (running obs normalization), [512,256,128] ELU, init_noise_std 1.0; Go1JoystickFlatTerrain max_iterations 1000 — [Playground locomotion_params.py (playground 0.2.0 wheel on PyPI)](https://pypi.org/project/playground/0.2.0/)
- **MuJoCo Playground Brax PPO config for Go1JoystickFlat/Rough**: num_timesteps 200M, normalize_observations True, unroll_length 20, num_minibatches 32, num_updates_per_batch 4, discounting 0.97, learning_rate 3e-4, entropy_cost 1e-2, num_envs 8192, batch_size 256, max_grad_norm 1.0, reward_scaling 1.0; policy (512,256,128) with obs key "state", value (512,256,128) with obs key **"privileged_state"** (asymmetric actor-critic) — [Playground locomotion_params.py](https://pypi.org/project/playground/0.2.0/)
- **Brax PPO train() library defaults** (v0.14.2): gae_lambda 0.95, clipping_epsilon 0.3, normalize_advantage True, discounting 0.9, entropy_cost 1e-4, lr 1e-4 (these are overridden by the Playground configs above) — [brax PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/brax/)
- **Training speed**: the Playground paper says most tasks need "only minutes of training time" on a single GPU, and the whole pipeline runs in a Colab notebook — [MuJoCo Playground arXiv 2502.08844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08844); [project page](https://playground.mujoco.org/). A Stanford CS123 lab reports about 20 minutes to train a Pupper velocity-tracking policy with Playground — [CS123 Lab 4](https://cs123-stanford.readthedocs.io/en/latest/schedule/labs/fall-26/lab-4.html)
- legged_gym (Rudin et al. 2021, "Learning to Walk in Minutes Using Massively Parallel Deep RL") is now superseded by Isaac Lab, and the repo "will receive limited updates" — [legged_gym README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leggedrobotics/legged_gym/master/README.md); paper [arXiv 2109.11978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11978)
- rsl_rl is now on PyPI as `rsl-rl-lib` and includes PPO, SAC, and student-teacher distillation, with multi-GPU support — [rsl_rl README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leggedrobotics/rsl_rl/main/README.md)
- **Go1 control timing in Playground**: ctrl_dt 0.02 (50 Hz policy), sim_dt 0.004 (5 substeps), episode_length 1000 steps (20 s), Kp 35, Kd 0.5, action_scale 0.5, history_len 1 — [Playground go1/joystick.py](https://pypi.org/project/playground/0.2.0/)

### Inferences
- **Recommended CPU starter config** (synthesized from the above, not taken from a single source): 50 Hz policy and 200–500 Hz physics; 16–64 MuJoCo envs (Gymnasium `AsyncVectorEnv`/SB3 `SubprocVecEnv`, or the native `mujoco.rollout` / threaded batch stepping); rollout of 24–64 steps per env per iteration; γ 0.99 (or 0.97 for shorter credit horizons), λ 0.95, clip 0.2, 5 epochs, 4–8 minibatches, lr 3e-4 to 1e-3 with adaptive-KL (target 0.01), entropy 0.001–0.01, max_grad_norm 1.0, running obs normalization, ELU MLP [256,128] or [512,256,128], init action std about 0.5–1.0 in normalized action units, actions clipped to about [-1,1] or a few std, then scaled by action_scale 0.25–0.5 rad around the default pose.
- With far fewer envs than the GPU setups, total samples matter more than wall-clock: the Go1 joystick uses about 200M steps on GPU. On CPU at roughly 10–50k steps/s, expect a usable flat-ground walk in roughly 10–50M steps. **Do not expect 200M on CPU.** CPG/residual priors and a simpler task (forward-velocity only) are the main levers to cut this down.
- The asymmetric critic (critic sees privileged state such as true lin vel, contact, applied push force, and friction) costs almost nothing and helps sample efficiency. Playground uses it for every Go1 task.
- The legged_gym "adaptive" schedule raises or lowers lr from the KL against desired_kl 0.01. It is robust when the lr starts high (1e-3), and useful when you cannot afford hyperparameter sweeps.

### Gaps
- I found no published benchmark of pure-CPU MuJoCo (non-MJX) PPO wall-clock for a quadruped. The CPU throughput estimates above are my extrapolation, not sourced.
- I could not read the Playground paper's appendix tables (arXiv blocked), so its exact wall-clock numbers for Go1 are unverified.

## Q2. CPG + residual RL vs PD-targets-around-nominal-pose; typical reward terms

### Takeaway
The dominant 2022–2026 practice is **joint PD targets = default_pose + action_scale·action** (Playground Go1, legged_gym), with dense shaped rewards built around exponential velocity tracking. CPG-based action spaces (CPG-RL: policy outputs oscillator amplitude/frequency per leg) are reported to be more sample-efficient and robust, especially under latency, but they need more custom code (oscillators plus foot-trajectory plus IK). Residual-over-CPG is a reasonable middle ground, but the evidence for its sample-efficiency gains is mixed.

### Cited Findings
- **Playground Go1 action mapping**: `motor_targets = default_pose + action * action_scale` (action_scale 0.5), with the default pose taken from the "home" keyframe and fed to position actuators (Kp 35, Kd 0.5) — [Playground go1/joystick.py](https://pypi.org/project/playground/0.2.0/)
- **Playground Go1 policy observation ("state", 48-D)**: noisy local linvel (3), noisy gyro (3), noisy gravity vector in body frame (3), joint angles minus default pose (12), joint vel (12), last action (12), command (3). The **privileged_state** for the critic adds clean versions plus accelerometer, actuator forces (12), last contacts (4), foot velocities (12), feet air time (4), the applied push force xfrc (3), and a perturbation-active flag — [Playground go1/joystick.py](https://pypi.org/project/playground/0.2.0/)
- **Playground Go1 observation noise** (uniform, ×level 1.0): joint_pos 0.03, joint_vel 1.5, gyro 0.2, gravity 0.05, linvel 0.1 — [Playground go1/joystick.py](https://pypi.org/project/playground/0.2.0/)
- **Playground Go1 reward scales** (tracking_sigma 0.25, max_foot_height 0.1): tracking_lin_vel +1.0, tracking_ang_vel +0.5, lin_vel_z −0.5, ang_vel_xy −0.05, orientation −5.0, dof_pos_limits −1.0, pose +0.5, termination −1.0, stand_still −1.0, torques −0.0002, action_rate −0.01, energy −0.001, feet_clearance −2.0, feet_height −0.2, feet_slip −0.1, feet_air_time +0.1 — [Playground go1/joystick.py](https://pypi.org/project/playground/0.2.0/)
- **Playground reward formulas**: tracking = `exp(-||cmd_xy - v_xy||² / sigma)`; orientation = sum of squared xy components of the torso z-axis; torques = `||τ||₂ + ||τ||₁`; energy = `Σ|qvel|·|actuator_force|`; action_rate = `Σ(a_t − a_{t−1})²`; pose = `exp(−Σ w·(q − q_default)²)`; stand_still = `Σ|q − q_default|` when |cmd| < 0.01; feet_slip = `Σ ||v_foot_xy||² · contact` (only when commanded to move); feet_air_time = `Σ (air_time − 0.1) · first_contact`, zero for a zero command — [Playground go1/joystick.py](https://pypi.org/project/playground/0.2.0/)
- **legged_gym reward scales**: tracking_lin_vel 1.0, tracking_ang_vel 0.5, lin_vel_z −2.0, ang_vel_xy −0.05, torques −1e-5, dof_acc −2.5e-7, feet_air_time 1.0, collision −1.0, action_rate −0.01 (orientation, base_height, stumble, and stand_still are 0 by default); tracking_sigma 0.25 — [legged_gym config](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leggedrobotics/legged_gym/master/legged_gym/envs/base/legged_robot_config.py). Its feet_air_time reward is `Σ(air_time − 0.5)·first_contact`, and it filters contacts with `contact OR last_contact` — [legged_gym legged_robot.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leggedrobotics/legged_gym/master/legged_gym/envs/base/legged_robot.py)
- **Gymnasium Ant-v5 baseline reward** (a simple alternative): healthy_reward 1.0 + forward_reward_weight 1·dx/dt − ctrl_cost_weight 0.5·||a||² − contact_cost_weight 5e-4·||F_contact||²; frame_skip 5 (dt 0.05); healthy_z_range (0.2, 1.0); contact_force_range (−1, 1) — [Gymnasium ant_v5.py (gymnasium 1.0.0)](https://pypi.org/project/gymnasium/1.0.0/). Ant learns "a walk" but is not a sim-to-real-style locomotion reward: it has no orientation, smoothness, or slip terms.
- **CPG-RL** (Bellegarda and Ijspeert, IEEE RA-L 2022): the policy modulates each limb oscillator's intrinsic amplitude and frequency (plus orientation in the omnidirectional version). The oscillator outputs map to foot positions, then to joint targets via IK and PD control, at a 100 Hz policy rate. Trained in sim and transferred to a Unitree A1, the policy handled an unseen 13.75 kg added load, and a policy can work with only contact booleans plus oscillator states as observations — [arXiv 2211.00458](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00458); [DeepAI summary](https://api.deepai.org/publication/cpg-rl-learning-central-pattern-generators-for-quadruped-locomotion)
- **Visual CPG-RL** (ICRA 2024, Go1): the CPG, explicit inter-oscillator couplings, and memory-enabled policies all improve energy efficiency, robustness, and tracking. Under 90 ms latency, CPG policies kept a 55–71% success rate while joint-space DRL baselines failed completely — [arXiv 2212.14400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.14400v2) (figures from search-result summaries; PDF not directly readable here)
- Mixed evidence on residual/CPG sample efficiency: one CPG-adaptation approach is reported as still sample-hungry (about 200M samples). A differentiable-sim study found that residual policies mainly improve asymptotic reward rather than sample efficiency in that setting. SYNLOCO (CDC 2024) adds RL residual feedback on top of a CPG gait planner — [Online Optimization of CPGs, arXiv 2410.16417](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16417v1); [Residual Policy Learning w/ Differentiable Sim](https://aitopics.org/doc/arxivorg:27FFDDD8)
- "Gait in Eight" (Bohlinger et al. 2025) compares joint-target prediction against a CPG architecture for sample-efficient on-robot learning with CrossQ — [TU Darmstadt PDF](https://www.ias.informatik.tu-darmstadt.de/uploads/Team/NicoBohlinger/gait_in_eight.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Practical recommendation for a small CPU stack**: start with PD targets around the nominal pose (Playground style), using the 48-D observation and a reduced reward set: tracking_lin_vel (exp), tracking_ang_vel, lin_vel_z, ang_vel_xy, orientation, torques/energy, action_rate, feet_slip, feet_air_time, termination. That set is proven and easy to implement in plain MuJoCo, since everything uses `data.sensordata`, `qfrc_actuator`, and contact checks.
- **For faster early learning**, add a **residual-over-CPG** option: compute a nominal trot from a 4-oscillator CPG (phase offsets of 0 and π for diagonal pairs) that produces foot trajectories or joint offsets, then set `q_target = q_default + q_cpg(φ) + action_scale_res·action` with a small residual scale (e.g. 0.1–0.25 rad). Optionally let the policy also modulate frequency and amplitude, as in CPG-RL. Include sin/cos of each leg's phase in the observation. The gait prior removes the need for feet_air_time and gait-shaping terms early on, and robots typically step from iteration 0. This is my design inference; I found no head-to-head wall-clock number.
- From my prior knowledge and **not verified in this session**, the CPG-RL oscillator is a Hopf-like amplitude/phase system: `r̈ = a(a/4(μ − r) − ṙ)`, `θ̇ = ω`. Foot x is `−d_step·r·cos θ`, and foot z is `−h + g_c·sin θ` in swing or `−h + g_p·sin θ` in stance. Verify against the paper before implementing.
- Use the exponential tracking kernel with sigma about 0.25. It keeps rewards bounded and positive, so early-termination exploits are less attractive. Keep the penalty-weight sum small relative to tracking. Playground's −5 orientation is fine because the squared tilt is small.

### Gaps
- I could not access CPG-RL's exact reward weights, PPO settings, or the training-sample comparison vs joint-space baselines (arXiv blocked).
- I found no source quantifying the wall-clock speedup of CPG+residual vs PD-around-nominal on CPU.

## Q3. Push perturbations, friction/mass randomization, motor strength/latency randomization

### Takeaway
There are two push styles. legged_gym **sets a random base velocity** every 15 s (|v_xy| ≤ 1.0 m/s). Playground applies a **half-sine force impulse** (xfrc_applied) to the torso, with random direction, magnitude 0–3 m/s equivalent, duration 0.05–0.2 s, and waits of 1–3 s. Standard dynamics DR: friction ~U(0.4–1.0) up to (0.05–4.5), link-mass scale ±10%, added torso mass ±1 kg (−1..+3), CoM shift ±5–10 cm, motor strength ×(0.9–1.1), Kp/Kd factors, joint offsets ±0.05 rad, action latency of up to about 6 control steps.

### Cited Findings
- **legged_gym push**: `_push_robots` "emulates an impulse by setting a randomized base velocity", with root lin vel x/y ~U(−max_push_vel_xy, +max_push_vel_xy); push_interval_s 15, max_push_vel_xy 1.0. Friction randomization is on with range [0.5, 1.25]; added_mass_range [−1, 1] (off by default) — [legged_gym legged_robot.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leggedrobotics/legged_gym/master/legged_gym/envs/base/legged_robot.py); [config](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leggedrobotics/legged_gym/master/legged_gym/envs/base/legged_robot_config.py)
- **legged_gym observation noise**: dof_pos 0.01, dof_vel 1.5, lin_vel 0.1, ang_vel 0.2, gravity 0.05, height 0.1 — [legged_gym config](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leggedrobotics/legged_gym/master/legged_gym/envs/base/legged_robot_config.py)
- **Playground Go1 push (pert_config)**: velocity_kick [0, 3.0] m/s, kick_durations [0.05, 0.2] s, kick_wait_times [1.0, 3.0] s, disabled by default (`enable=False`), random horizontal direction. Force profile is `F(t) = 0.5·sin(π t / T) · m_torso · v_kick / T`, written into `data.xfrc_applied[torso, :3]` and zeroed between kicks. The applied force and a "perturbation active" flag go into the critic's privileged state — [Playground go1/joystick.py](https://pypi.org/project/playground/0.2.0/)
- A Playground Go1 joystick policy was shown recovering from a kick while moving at about 2 m/s on hardware — [MuJoCo Playground paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08844) (figure caption via search summary)
- **Playground Go1 domain randomization** (per-env model params): floor friction ~U(0.4, 1.0); joint frictionloss ×U(0.9, 1.1); armature ×U(1.0, 1.05); torso CoM shift +U(−0.05, 0.05) m; all link masses ×U(0.9, 1.1); torso added mass +U(−1, 1) kg; qpos0 (joint zero offset) +U(−0.05, 0.05) rad — [Playground go1/randomize.py](https://pypi.org/project/playground/0.2.0/)
- **walk-these-ways Go1 DR** (Margolis and Agrawal): added_mass_range [−1, 3], friction_range [0.05, 4.5], restitution [0, 1], com_displacement [−0.1, 0.1], **motor_strength_range [0.9, 1.1]**, Kp_factor [0.8, 1.3] and Kd_factor [0.5, 1.5] (off by default), rand_interval_s 6 (re-randomize during episodes), gravity_range [−1, 1], push_interval_s 15. Base config has **randomize_lag_timesteps True, lag_timesteps 6** (action latency buffer) and motor_offset_range [−0.05, 0.05] — [walk-these-ways go1_config.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Improbable-AI/walk-these-ways/master/go1_gym/envs/go1/go1_config.py); [walk-these-ways legged_robot_config.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Improbable-AI/walk-these-ways/master/go1_gym/envs/base/legged_robot_config.py)
- Latency robustness is a reported strength of CPG action spaces: CPG policies tolerate 90 ms delay where joint-space baselines fail — [Visual CPG-RL arXiv 2212.14400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.14400v2)

### Inferences
- **Implementation in plain MuJoCo (CPU)**:
  - Friction: `model.geom_friction[floor_id, 0] = U(...)`.
  - Mass: `model.body_mass[i] *= U(0.9, 1.1)`, plus torso `+U(−1, 1)`.
  - CoM: `model.body_ipos[torso] += U(±0.05)`.
  - Motor strength: scale `model.actuator_gainprm[:,0]` together with `biasprm[:,1]` for position actuators (Kp factor), or scale the torque after a manual PD.
  - Latency: keep a ring buffer of the last k actions and apply `action[t − lag]` with lag ~U{0..k}. At 50 Hz, k = 1–2 covers 20–40 ms.
  - Push: either set `data.qvel[0:2] += U(±1)` every N seconds (legged_gym style, simplest), or apply a half-sine `xfrc_applied` impulse (Playground style, more physical).
  - With separate `MjModel` copies per env, randomize per reset. Re-randomizing mid-episode (walk-these-ways `rand_interval_s`) adds robustness.
- **Curriculum**: start without pushes and DR for the first ~20–30% of training, then ramp push magnitude (e.g. 0 to 1–3 m/s). Neither config above does this by default; it is a common practice inference.
- **For a "robustness platform"**, log success rate vs push magnitude and direction as the evaluation metric. The Playground kick parameterization (magnitude in m/s and duration) is a clean, physically interpretable axis for a robustness sweep.

- I did not verify how motor-strength scaling is applied in walk-these-ways code (torque scaling vs gain scaling); only the config ranges were confirmed.
- Exact latency handling in Playground's real Go1 deployment was not checked.

## Q4. Viewing MuJoCo sims in a browser

### Takeaway
DeepMind now ships **official MuJoCo WebAssembly bindings** (`@mujoco/mujoco` on npm). zalo/mujoco_wasm has become a three.js demo suite on top of them (MuJoCo 3.12.0 per its README). You can either (a) run the physics in the browser and do policy inference in JS (ONNX Runtime Web, as in mjswan), or (b) run the sim and policy in Python and stream `xpos`/`xquat` per body over WebSocket to a three.js scene that loads the same geometry.

### Cited Findings
- zalo/mujoco_wasm: "Load and Run MuJoCo 3.12.0 Models using JavaScript and the official MuJoCo WebAssembly Bindings". Since DeepMind completed the official bindings, the project "is now just a small demo suite in the `examples` folder". `npm install` pulls three.js and the official WASM bindings, and it ships a live demo. The API is `import load_mujoco from "@mujoco/mujoco"`; write MJCF into an Emscripten MEMFS (`mujoco.FS.writeFile`); `mujoco.MjModel.mj_loadXML(path)`; `new mujoco.MjData(model)`; typed-array views `data.qpos/qvel/ctrl/xpos`; `mujoco.mj_step(model, data)`; `mj_forward`; `mj_resetData`; `mj_applyFT(...)` for pushes; call `.delete()` to free memory — [zalo/mujoco_wasm README](https://github.com/zalo/mujoco_wasm); [live demo](https://zalo.github.io/mujoco_wasm/)
- Official npm package `@mujoco/mujoco` includes the precompiled WASM module, JS bindings, and TypeScript declarations; bundlers must serve the `.wasm` file correctly — [@mujoco/mujoco README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@mujoco/mujoco@3.9.0/README.md) (via search summary)
- MuJoCo is Z-up and three.js is Y-up, so the mujoco_wasm demos include coordinate-conversion helpers (MuJoCo y maps to three.js −z) — [mujoco_wasm (search summary)](https://github.com/zalo/mujoco_wasm)
- **mjswan / muwanx**: uses MuJoCo WASM for physics, ONNX Runtime Web for policy inference, and three.js for rendering, and is deployable as a static site — [muwanx docs](https://muwanx.readthedocs.io/); [PyPI muwanx](https://pypi.org/project/muwanx/0.0.7/)
- Other options: MuJoCo-GS-Web (MuJoCo WASM plus Gaussian splat rendering) — [World Labs showcase](https://www.worldlabs.ai/labs/showcase/mujoco-gs-web); a three.js-based viewer package `@likang233/mujoco-viewer` — [pkgstats](https://www.pkgstats.com/pkg:@likang233/mujoco-viewer); a React component `@vuer-ai/mujoco-ts` — [jsDelivr README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@vuer-ai/mujoco-ts@0.0.79/README.md); RoboPianist's browser demo as an "advanced example" — [kzakka.com/robopianist](https://kzakka.com/robopianist/)
- The MuJoCo Playground RSS 2025 demo page hosts in-browser interactive demos — [playground.mujoco.org/demo](https://playground.mujoco.org/demo/) (page not readable here; existence from search results)

### Inferences
- **Simplest architecture for a Python training stack plus a browser viewer** (path b): a Python server (FastAPI/aiohttp/websockets) steps `mujoco.MjData` at real time, and each frame sends a compact binary message (Float32Array of `nbody × (3 pos + 4 quat)`, about 13 bodies × 28 B ≈ 364 B/frame at 30–60 Hz). The browser builds three.js meshes once from geom info (send `model.geom_type/size/rgba/bodyid` and local `geom_pos/quat` as JSON at connect, or export meshes as glTF), then updates `Object3D.position/quaternion` per body. Convert MuJoCo quaternions (w,x,y,z) to three.js (x,y,z,w) and apply the Z-up to Y-up rotation on a root group. User actions (push button, change friction) go back over the same socket and call `mj_applyFT`/`xfrc_applied` server-side. This keeps the exact training physics and the PyTorch policy authoritative.
- **Path (a), fully in-browser**: export the policy to ONNX and run it with onnxruntime-web next to `@mujoco/mujoco`. You get no server and a static hosting option, but the obs construction and PD/DR code must be reimplemented in JS, a risk of sim mismatch. Pin the same MuJoCo version in Python and JS.
