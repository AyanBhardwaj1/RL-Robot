How it’s counted

A fall is tilt past 65° or the body within 11 cm of the ground. A stall is under 35% of the commanded distance. Both count as failures.

Fair questions

Is this a real robot?

No. It’s a generic 6 kg, 12-joint quadruped built procedurally in MuJoCo. Its numbers show relative robustness between policies, not certified limits of any product.

What does “breaking point” mean exactly?

The largest stress the policy still passes on every seed, where passing means no fall and at least 35% of the commanded distance. It’s found by bisection, so it’s accurate to the last halving step.

Why should I trust a 0% failure rate?

You shouldn’t read it alone. Every rate comes with a 95% upper bound: 0 failures in 30 worlds means the true rate is at most 9.5%. The report always states how hard it searched.

Can I test my own policy?

Yes. Point --policy at py:your.module:factory, returning any callable from observation to action, or train in Breakpoint’s worlds through its Gymnasium environment.

Do I need an LLM or an API key?

No. Descriptions like “icy ramp, 4 kg box, shoved from the left” are parsed by rules offline. Any OpenAI-compatible endpoint, such as a Llama API, can be plugged in, and its output is clamped to physical bounds.

What does the latency gate actually do?

It fades the learned correction out as measured control lag rises from 40 to 80 ms, falling back to the open-loop gait, which never reads stale sensors. Your stack has to measure its own link latency.

How long does a full audit take?

About a minute per policy on four CPU cores: 17 breaking points, 24 nominal runs, an adversarial hunt, compound and red-team tests, and 150 deployment worlds.
