Which spec change helps most

One spec improves by a realistic step; every job is re-run on the same 750 sampled shifts, so each shift either flips from a fall to a finish (fixed), the other way (broken), or stays put. The sign test on those flips says whether the gain is real (python -m breakpoint design).

How it decides where to step

The centre of mass moves like an inverted pendulum over the centre of pressure p: x″ = ω²(x − p), with ω = √(g / z₀). Its capture point ξ = x + ẋ/ω is where the robot must put its centre of pressure to come to rest, and it runs away from p: ξ′ = ω(ξ − p).

Ankles first: the centre of pressure goes to the point of the support nearest ξ, capped by ankle torque and by friction (|x − p| ≤ μz). If ξ leaves the support, the robot steps where ξ will be at touchdown, capped by leg reach, lateral reach and swing time; while one foot swings, only the other carries weight, which is why side shoves are hardest. More than 3 steps, or a slip, is a fall.

Latency: the controller measures ξ late, but it knows the centre of pressure it has commanded since, so it rolls the measurement forward (a Smith predictor). What lag costs is the time a shove goes unseen, about a factor e^(−ωL) on the largest survivable shove.

The wheeled base has a big footprint and no steps: it drives under the capture point with bounded acceleration and speed, and traction falls with friction. It tips over if ξ escapes faster than it can follow, and is blocked by anything taller than a threshold.
